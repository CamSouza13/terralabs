SPACE LIFE SUPPORT / VALIDATED IN SIMULATION

Keeping a crew alive, off Earth.

Bioregenerative life support is the hardest closed living system there is, and a NASA-documented gap. It is Terra's north star, and where we have the most rigorous validation to date.

The validation, in numbers

We ran Terra against SIMOC, the agent-based closed-ecosystem model developed with Biosphere 2, across a 60-run ensemble spanning seven habitat configurations and dozens of fault conditions. In each run Terra saw only two gas sensors, calibrated itself to that habitat, and forecast a crew-safety breach.

This is simulation-to-simulation validation against an independent, peer-built model. It is not flight data, and Terra has no flight heritage. The two false alarms and one low-lead case are reported in the full write-up rather than hidden.

Why the north star pulls the whole company

Bioregenerative life support, growing food and regenerating air in a sealed habitat, is a NASA-documented gap and the hardest closed living system there is. An engine hardened on that problem is exactly what makes the aquaculture product trustworthy on Earth. Same math, same audit-logged autonomy, three closed worlds.

Where this stands

Simulation-validated today, pre-revenue, no flight heritage. We are looking for research partners and non-dilutive support (for example USDA, NOAA, and NASA SBIR tracks) to move from simulation toward bench validation.
